Medication Guide & Symptom Checker

Review your prescribed treatments below to understand how they work and what side effects you should monitor according to clinical practice guidelines.

📊 Clinical Baseline: Uric acid typically begins to crystallize and deposit in joints at levels above 7.0 mg/dL (416 µmol/L). Long-term preventative therapies aim to keep baseline levels well below this threshold to prevent crystal accumulation.

🔴 Acute Flare Management (Pain & Inflammation)

Used directly during an active gout attack to reduce severe pain and joint swelling.

Colchicine
Anti-inflammatory Microtubule Inhibitor
Symptoms to report: Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, and stomach cramps. Infrequently: headache and fatigue.
Guideline recommendation: Lower doses (1.2 mg followed by 0.6 mg 1 hour later) are as effective as higher doses for reducing pain and are associated with significantly fewer gastrointestinal adverse effects.
NSAIDs (e.g., Indomethacin, Naproxen, Ibuprofen)
Nonsteroidal Anti-inflammatory Drugs
Symptoms to report: Indigestion, heartburn (dyspepsia), or severe stomach pain. Report any signs of gastrointestinal bleeding or ulcers immediately.
Guideline recommendation: Strong recommendation with high-quality evidence supporting use. Different types of NSAIDs show no clinically important differences in efficacy when treating gout flares.
Corticosteroids (e.g., Prednisolone)
Systemic Glucocorticoids
Symptoms to report: Mood changes, dysphoria, fluid retention (swelling), or elevated blood sugar levels.
Guideline recommendation: Equivalent to NSAIDs for managing acute gout but generally safer for patients with kidney disease, heart failure, or cirrhosis where NSAIDs are contraindicated.

🔵 Chronic Preventative Therapy (Urate-Lowering)

Taken daily over the long term to lower uric acid levels in the blood and prevent future attacks.

Allopurinol
Xanthine Oxidase Inhibitor
Symptoms to report: Skin rash. Seek immediate medical attention if a rash develops, as it can indicate a rare but serious hypersensitivity reaction.
Guideline recommendation: Highly effective at decreasing serum urate levels. Long-term therapy is typically recommended for patients experiencing recurrent attacks (2 or more per year).
Febuxostat
Xanthine Oxidase Inhibitor
Symptoms to report: Abdominal pain, diarrhea, and musculoskeletal or joint aches.
Guideline recommendation: Equally effective as Allopurinol at dropping serum urate levels. Often utilized as an alternative long-term preventative option.
